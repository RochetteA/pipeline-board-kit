01 / HOW IT WORKS

A robot that works in your sheet

This edition is for team members running Claude Code. If that's not you, the no-code path gets you the identical board in two minutes.

Here's the trick that lets Claude do the work instead of handing you code: Google lets you create a robot account (Google calls it a "service account") that can edit a spreadsheet you share with it, exactly like a teammate. Your Claude walks you through creating one (about 5 minutes of clicking in the Google Cloud console, once), you share your sheet with the robot's email address, & from then on your Claude builds tabs, writes formulas, sets colors & dropdowns, & imports leads directly. Nothing is ever pasted into the spreadsheet.

Create the robot (once)→ Share your sheet with it→ Claude builds the board→ Claude imports your leads
Your Claude doesGuides the robot setup, reads your lead tab's columns itself (no downloads, no Drive connection), builds the board to your stages & your form, imports your leads, tidies old lists, re-syncs, fixes problems.
You doAbout ten clicks in the Google Cloud console, one Share, & answering questions. Claude narrates every step & waits for you.

Sheet owned by someone else? That's fine. You only need to share it with the robot's email as an Editor. If you can't share it yourself, the owner adds that one address & you're off; it takes them ten seconds.

About the robot's key file. Creating the robot gives you a small .json file: think of it as that robot's password. Keep it in your project folder, never post or send it, & you can delete the robot in Google Cloud any time. It can only touch sheets you've shared with it.

02 / BEFORE YOU START

What you need

  • Claude Code, installed & signed in, opened in a fresh empty folder (it will save a couple of files there).
  • A Google account to create the robot with. Any Google account works; the one that uses the sheet is simplest.
  • Your spreadsheet: the KPI tracker your leads land in, or a blank new sheet. You need to be able to share it (or know who can).
  • 20 to 30 minutes the first time. Most of it is answering questions.

No Meta connection, no Google Drive connection, no downloads: the robot reads your lead tab directly. & if "Claude Code" doesn't ring a bell, take the no-code path instead.

03 / BUILD IT

The build prompt

Copy this & paste it into Claude Code. It creates the robot with you, then interviews you, then builds. Nothing happens without you seeing it first.

Prompt · Build my pipeline board
You are setting up my "Pipeline Board": a Trello-style lead pipeline built directly inside my Google Sheet, using my team's kit. I may not be technical. Work with me one step at a time and wait for my answer before moving on. Never ask me to paste code anywhere, and never touch Apps Script; everything goes into the sheet through the kit's builder tool and a Google robot account.

STEP A: the tool. Download my team's builder from https://rochettea.github.io/pipeline-board-kit/pipeline-board-builder.js into this folder and read its header comment; it explains every command and the config file. Check that Node is installed (node -v, version 18 or newer). If it is missing, walk me through installing it from nodejs.org (LTS), then continue.

STEP B: the robot account, one click at a time, confirming after each step. Explain in one sentence what a service account is (a robot that can edit sheets I share with it; it can only touch what I share). Then guide me: 1) open console.cloud.google.com and sign in (accept the terms if it is my first visit); 2) the project picker at the top, "New project", name it Pipeline Board, Create, then select it; 3) menu, APIs and Services, Library, search "Google Sheets API", open it, Enable; 4) menu, IAM and Admin, Service Accounts, Create service account, name it pipeline-board-robot, Create and continue, Continue (no roles needed), Done; 5) click the robot in the list, Keys tab, Add key, Create new key, JSON, Create, which downloads a .json file; 6) have me move that file into this folder and rename it service-account.json. Read the file yourself, tell me the robot's email address (client_email), and remind me the file is like a password: keep it here, never share it. Add it to a .gitignore file in this folder.

STEP C: my sheet. Ask me for the link to the spreadsheet I want the board in, or tell me I can start a blank Google Sheet at sheets.new if I prefer a fresh one. Then have me share that sheet with the robot's email as an Editor (Share button, paste the address, untick Notify). If the sheet belongs to someone else and I cannot share it, tell me to ask the owner to add that address. Write pipeline-config.json with the sheet link, then run: node pipeline-board-builder.js check. Keep helping me until check passes.

STEP D: interview me, one question at a time. 1) Keep the default pipeline stages, or use my own list? The first stage is where every new lead lands. If I give custom stages, ask which ones mean "no follow-up owed" and choose colors so they still read as one progression. 2) How many questions does my instant form have (3, 4, 5, or none), and what are they? Set answerHeaders to one short header per question; if I say "generic" or have no form, use Form Q1, Q2, Q3 and I can rename them on the sheet later. 3) Do leads land automatically on a tab in that spreadsheet (from a Meta instant form or a form tool)? If yes, ask for the tab name exactly as written at the bottom of the sheet, run: node pipeline-board-builder.js inspect "That Tab Name", read the output yourself, work out the column numbers for name, email, and each answer, and whether the data starts on row 1 or 2. Put that in leadTabs in the config. If no lead tab, leave leadTabs empty.

STEP E: build. Save the config, then run: node pipeline-board-builder.js build. If I have a lead tab, then run: node pipeline-board-builder.js import, and tell me how many people came over (they all show as DUE on purpose, until I touch their card). Then run: node pipeline-board-builder.js due. Give me the board link the build printed and ask me to open it and tell you what I see: the colored stage columns, the count row, and my people as cards. If anything looks wrong, fix it with me.

STEP F: how to use it, briefly. Moving someone is the Stage dropdown on the Pipeline tab; the board redraws itself. When I follow up with someone, I type today's date in Last Touch (or a specific date in Next FU) and the DUE flag and the warning mark on their card clear until the follow-up is due again. Never type in the Due? column. Remind me that "node pipeline-board-builder.js import" pulls new leads over any time, and that this manual has a prompt to automate that.

What to expect

Claude will stop after every step & wait for you: "tell me when you've clicked Enable", "paste me the link to your sheet", "what do you see on the board?". The Google Cloud part looks more intimidating than it is; it's a handful of menu clicks & one download, & Claude knows exactly where each button lives. The moment "check" passes, you're past the only fiddly part. Everything after that is Claude doing the work.

04 / YOUR LEADS · OPTIONAL

Move your existing leads over

Separate on purpose: the board works from minute one without it. If your leads land on a tab in the same spreadsheet, the build prompt already offered to import them. Use this one for everything else, or to do it later:

Prompt · Move my existing leads
My Pipeline Board is already built in my Google Sheet with my team's builder tool (pipeline-board-builder.js and pipeline-config.json are in this folder, and the robot account works). Help me bring my existing leads onto the board. Ask me first: where do my leads live today?

Case 1: on a tab inside the SAME spreadsheet as the board. Ask for the exact tab name, run: node pipeline-board-builder.js inspect "That Tab Name", work out the column numbers for name, email, and each answer, add it to leadTabs in pipeline-config.json, then run: node pipeline-board-builder.js import. Tell me how many people came over. Remind me this command is safe to run again any time: it only adds people who are not on the board yet.

Case 2: anywhere else (a Trello board, another spreadsheet, a CSV export, my notes, my phone). I will paste the list or drop files in this folder, in whatever messy format I have. Turn it into a clean CSV in this folder with this header row: name,email, then one column per answer header in my config (blank is fine), then stage,last_touch. Map each person to the closest stage in my pipeline (default: the first stage). Leave last_touch blank so everyone flags as due for a check-in, unless my data includes a real last-contact date (use yyyy-mm-dd). Show me the first few rows so I can sanity-check the names, then run: node pipeline-board-builder.js import-csv thatfile.csv. People already on the board are skipped automatically.

Afterward, run: node pipeline-board-builder.js due, and ask me to glance at the count row on the Pipeline Board tab.
05 / FORM QUESTIONS

Three, four, or five questions

Instant forms differ across the team. The builder's config has answerHeaders, one entry per question, & every column in the sheet repositions itself around however many you have. If you covered this in the build interview, you're done. Use this when your form changes, or you skipped it the first time:

Prompt · Match my form questions
My Pipeline Board is built with my team's builder tool (pipeline-board-builder.js and pipeline-config.json are in this folder). My instant form has [3 / 4 / 5] questions. Here they are: [paste your questions].

Update answerHeaders in pipeline-config.json to one short header per question, and if leadTabs is set, update its answerCols to match (run inspect on the lead tab if you need to see the columns). The sheet layout adapts to the count automatically.

Then rebuild without losing anyone: first run: node pipeline-board-builder.js export backup.csv, and tell me how many people it saved. Then run: node pipeline-board-builder.js build --rebuild. Then run: node pipeline-board-builder.js import-csv backup.csv, and confirm the same number of people came back, with their stages and dates intact. Finish with: node pipeline-board-builder.js due.
06 / KEEPING IT CURRENT

New leads & the daily list

Because the robot works from your computer rather than living inside the sheet, two small things are yours to run, & your Claude can put both on a schedule:

  • New leads come over whenever import runs. Run it yourself, or have Claude schedule it (every hour, every morning, whatever you like).
  • Who's due today comes from due. Same deal: run it, or let Claude schedule it & show you the list each morning.
  • Follow-up dates: when you touch someone, type today's date in Last Touch (or the real date in Next FU). The DUE flags & ⚠ marks update on their own.
Prompt · Keep my board current automatically
My Pipeline Board is built with my team's builder tool (pipeline-board-builder.js and pipeline-config.json in this folder). Set it up so my computer keeps the board current on its own: run "node pipeline-board-builder.js import" every hour, and run "node pipeline-board-builder.js due" every morning at 8 with the result saved to a file called due-today.txt in this folder that I can open. Use the right scheduler for my operating system (Task Scheduler on Windows, launchd on a Mac), make it survive restarts, and use full file paths so it works when I am not in this folder. Then show me how to check that it ran, how to read the morning list, and how to turn it off. Note that it only runs while my computer is awake, and tell me how to run either command by hand any time.

Want the board to do even more from inside the sheet (dates that stamp themselves when you change a stage, an email instead of a file)? The no-code template's built-in engine does that, & the two can coexist. Ask Ashley if you want it; it's a separate walkthrough.

07 / WHEN LIFE HAPPENS

Fixes

Your troubleshooter lives on your computer & can see everything. Hand it the problem:

Prompt · Fix my board
My Pipeline Board (built with my team's builder tool, pipeline-board-builder.js and pipeline-config.json in this folder) is misbehaving. Here is what I see: [describe it, paste any error text, or describe the screenshot]. Start by running: node pipeline-board-builder.js check, and read pipeline-config.json. Common causes to rule out in order: the robot is not shared on the sheet as an Editor (check prints the exact address to share with); the sheet link in the config changed; a tab was renamed; the answer count in the config does not match the board. A fresh copy of the builder is always at https://rochettea.github.io/pipeline-board-kit/pipeline-board-builder.js if mine got damaged. Diagnose it with me step by step, fix what you can yourself, and tell me plainly when something needs a click from me.

Still stuck?

Prompt your Claude, in plain words: "I'm stuck. Here's what I see: [describe it or paste a screenshot]. Walk me through getting unstuck, one small step at a time." It has the whole picture right there on your machine, & it will keep going with you until things work.

& if the two of you are truly stumped, message Ashley with a screenshot & what your Claude tried. Every snag that reaches her makes both manuals better for the next person.
